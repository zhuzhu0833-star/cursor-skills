# Narrative Spine, Hooks, Style, Outline

Do this after the story is chosen. The spine is the argument. The outline is how the argument is staged. Neither is a table of contents of the CV.

## Spine

Write these before paragraphs exist:

- **Central question.** What they actually want to understand.
- **Central tension.** The contradiction they cannot yet settle.
- **Recurring pattern.** What keeps returning across experiences.
- **Turning point.** Which experience changed direction.
- **Knowledge gap.** What they cannot do or know yet.
- **Graduate need.** Why a degree, now, is the way through that gap.
- **Future direction.** Where they want to take the question.

The chain is: experience → pattern → question → exploration → gap → graduate study → future.

## Why major

Do not write "this experience sparked my interest in X" unless the next sentences earn the spark. The only acceptable bridge is:

concrete experience → observed problem → changed understanding → intellectual question → need for the discipline → graduate study.

If a step is missing, the motive is a label. Go back and interview, or cut the claim.

## Why program

"The program offers excellent courses" is not a reason. Ask first what the student cannot solve, then which resource in this program is the one that addresses it: a method, a lab, a dataset, a faculty question, a kind of training. The chain is: my question → my gap → this resource → the specific capacity it builds → how that returns to the work.

Name a resource only when the student supplied it or you have looked it up and marked the source. Do not invent faculty fit.

## Hook selector

Draft 3–5 openings and compare them. Priority is specificity, personal voice, and academic potential. Drama is not a tie-breaker.

| Hook | Enters through |
| --- | --- |
| A. Scene | A concrete moment |
| B. Problem | A specific problem |
| C. Paradox | Something that should not both be true |
| D. Observation | A detail the student noticed |
| E. Reflective | An understanding already formed |

Recommend one. Say which academic job it does in the first paragraph, and what the others would cost.

## Style

Recommend one primary style. Offer the others only as real alternatives given this file, not as a menu you always print in full.

| Style | Pressure | Fits when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Cinematic | Scene, detail, a sense of a person | The file has a strong scene and a voice |
| B. Intellectual | Question, contradiction, research interest | Economics, policy, social science, research-heavy STEM |
| C. Problem-driven | Problem, move, limit, larger problem | CS, AI, engineering, data, business |
| D. Reflective | Experience, long observation, academic interest | Humanities, arts, or a background that is itself the material |

Story should not automatically outweigh scholarship. Shift the mix by program:

- Research-heavy: more question, method, reflection.
- Professional: more problem, action, application.
- Interdisciplinary: more connection, pattern, intellectual bridge.
- Humanities and social science: more observation, interpretation, question.
- Creative or arts-related: more scene, voice, perception, artistic motive.

## Outline

For each paragraph record:

- **Function.** Why the paragraph exists.
- **Evidence.** Which real experience supports it.
- **Reflection.** What the student is understanding here.
- **Transition.** Why the next paragraph follows from this one, not from the word "therefore."

A paragraph that could be deleted without damaging Why Major is background. Cut it, compress it, or merge it.
