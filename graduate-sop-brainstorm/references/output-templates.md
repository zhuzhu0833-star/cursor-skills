# Output templates

Use these shapes so the student can respond. Keep internal scores and the full graph out of the message unless they ask for the diagnosis. Write interview questions in the student's language.

## After profile, story bank, and depth diagnosis

```markdown
## What I see
[6–10 lines: who they are, the pattern that might be forming, the gap.]

## Stories worth pursuing
1. **Title** — scene in one sentence. Why it might matter. What is still missing (one layer).
2. ...

## This round
I am asking about [experience], because [the missing layer] is what would make it an essay rather than a CV line.

1. ...
2. ...
3. ...
```

Three to five questions. No extra questionnaire after them.

## After mining and strategy

```markdown
## Narrative spine
- Central question:
- Central tension:
- Recurring pattern:
- Turning point:
- Knowledge gap:
- Graduate need:
- Future direction:

## Hook options
1. **Type** — two or three sentences of the opening. What it buys, what it costs.
2. ...

Recommendation: [one hook], because [specificity / voice / academic job].

## Style
Recommended: [A/B/C/D], because [material and program type].
Alternative: [only if it is genuinely viable].
```

## Outline

```markdown
## Paragraph 1
- Function:
- Evidence:
- Reflection:
- Transition:
```

Repeat for each paragraph. Then stop if the user has not asked you to draft yet and a choice of hook or style is still open. If they asked for the essay, continue.

## Draft delivery

The essay first, with a working title only if useful. No outline repeated above it.

Under the essay, a compact audit:

```markdown
## Audit
- Cut or compressed:
- Claims narrowed:
- Still open: 【Evidence Missing】 or 【Interview Vulnerability】 items, or "none."
```

## Final revision

The revised essay, then three to six sentences on what changed in the causal chain. Do not attach a score.
