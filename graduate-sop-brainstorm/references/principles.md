# Principles

These outrank structure, drama, and polish.

## Truth over story

Narrative shape and sentence-level editing are allowed. Manufacturing facts is not. Do not invent scenes, dialogue, emotions, results, research contributions, difficulties, personal background, or an inner state the student never expressed. If a draft needs a fact you do not have, ask or mark **【Evidence Missing】**. A plausible inference is still an invention when it is written as something that happened.

## Thinking over achievement

A graduate essay is not "what I did." It is "what doing these things slowly taught me to ask." An award, a famous lab, or a long task list is raw material. It becomes an essay only when it changes what the student understands.

## Causality over chronology

Do not order paragraphs because A happened before B. Prefer: A changed how I understood X, therefore I pursued B, B revealed Y, therefore I now need to study Z. The whole piece should show that experiences change thinking, thinking changes choices, and choices lead to graduate study.

## What to look for, in order

1. The problem inside the story.
2. The change in understanding inside the problem.
3. The academic motive inside that change.
4. Only then, why this program.

Do not treat the essay as "my experiences plus why I am applying."

## What the committee should see

A person who has real experience, notices things, finds problems, makes judgments, reflects, keeps asking, and is forming a direction. Standards, in this order: truth before finish, the specific before the grand, the question before the label, thinking before achievement, cause before list, a personal voice before template language, academic motive before enthusiasm for a major name, real complexity before a perfect persona.

## Depth score is not a grade

Use D0–D6 only to decide where to interview. Never tell the student the essay "scores" a D-level, and never rank them against other applicants.

| Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| D0 | Fact only |
| D1 | Concrete action |
| D2 | A personal decision |
| D3 | A tension or conflict |
| D4 | A change in understanding |
| D5 | A question that persists |
| D6 | That question naturally requires this graduate field |

## Running rules

- Enough material: go to the next stage.
- Thin material: interview. Do not fill the gap yourself.
- High-value new information: abandon the old question path and rebuild the hypothesis.
- Shared theme across experiences: one pattern, not three essays.
- Contradiction: test it as intellectual tension before resolving it.
- Impressive but thoughtless: leave it out.
- Ordinary but intellectually consequential: consider it first.
- Finished draft: check the causal chain from experience to graduate need, not only the sentences.
