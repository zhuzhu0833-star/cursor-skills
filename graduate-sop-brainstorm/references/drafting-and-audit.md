# Drafting and Admissions Audit

## Draft

Write Concrete → Reflective → Analytical → Forward-looking. Do not write Abstract → Generic → Inspirational.

English, when the submission is in English: natural, mature, precise, intellectually curious, restrained, personal. Avoid cliché, inflated ambition, adjective piles, corporate jargon, admissions-template phrases ("ever since I was young," "passionate about," "hone my skills," "make a difference"), and sophistication that is only there to sound educated.

Use the student's real details as the diction. If a sentence could sit in another applicant's essay unchanged, it is not finished.

Do not announce interest. Show the problem that made the interest unavoidable.

## AI-tone marks

After the first draft, mark and then rewrite:

- **Generic.** True of any student.
- **Announcing.** Says "I am interested" and stops.
- **Resume.** Lists an experience instead of thinking through it.
- **Decorative.** Sounds good, adds no information.
- **Overclaiming.** The conclusion is larger than the evidence.
- **Forced transition.** The next paragraph arrives only because of "therefore" or "this sparked."
- **AI-like pattern.** Too symmetrical, stock scaffolding, no personal wording.

## Only-this-student test

For each paragraph: if the name were swapped, would the paragraph still hold? If yes, it needs a more specific detail, a more personal judgment, a real difficulty, a stranger observation, or a clearer change of mind. Do not fix this by adding adjectives.

## Deletion test

If removing the paragraph leaves the Why Major logic standing, it is optional. Cut, compress, or merge.

## Interview stress test

Read the finished draft as a committee member and ask, of every important claim: Why? Why did you do that? How exactly? What surprised you? What did you learn? Why does that matter? Why this field? Why graduate school? Why now? Why this program?

Any conclusion that cannot survive those questions is **【Interview Vulnerability】**. Do not patch it with a more confident sentence. Return to the material, ask, or narrow the claim to what the evidence can hold.

## Audits to run before the revision

1. **Specificity.** Scenes, numbers, decisions, and objects are present where they matter.
2. **Causality.** Order follows changes in understanding, not the CV timeline.
3. **Reflection.** Each major episode changes a belief.
4. **Why major.** The six-step bridge is intact.
5. **Why program.** Gap matches a real resource.
6. **Cliché.** Template phrases are gone.
7. **AI tone.** The marks above are rewritten.
8. **Interview stress test.** Vulnerabilities are either fixed with evidence or the claim is reduced.

## Revision

The last version must still be one chain: an experience started a question, later experiences were how the student explored it, the student can now see what they do not know, and graduate training is the systematic next step. Language edits that break that chain are not improvements.

When you deliver the revision, include a short audit note: what was cut, which claim was narrowed, and any **【Evidence Missing】** or **【Interview Vulnerability】** that remains because the student has not answered yet.
